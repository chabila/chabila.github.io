A BA practice case study

Migrating from SAP Commerce Cloud to Centra

Moving from a monolithic, all-in-one commerce suite to a headless platform is less a lift-and-shift than a decision about what the business still wants. This is how I would run it as the BA: what to scope, how to map, who owns what, and what to do when the plan meets reality.

Chabila Borkar · Senior Business Analyst, Payments & eCommerce
How to read this

This is an illustrative multi-brand, multi-market scenario, not a claim that I have delivered a Centra migration. The SAP Commerce Cloud side comes from years of delivery on it; the Centra side is based on Centra's public developer documentation. Treat the Centra specifics as the starting point for discovery, not a substitute for it.

Part 1

Defining scope

Centra is headless: it has no CMS, templates or themes, and exposes everything through APIs. SAP Commerce Cloud bundled several things Centra will not, so the first scoping job is finding what silently needs a new home.

BucketExamplesScope decision
In scope: migratesProducts, variants, prices, stock, customers, markets, campaigns, payment and shipping configMapped, loaded, reconciled
Needs a new ownerContent management, site search, storefront front end, URL redirectsNot in Centra; needs a CMS, search tool and frontend build, each with an owner and budget
DeferredFull order history older than an agreed window, rarely used promotionsArchive or phase 2, signed off by Finance and Legal
Out of scopeERP redesign, new loyalty schemeLogged in change control, not absorbed
The most expensive scope mistake is the capability nobody listed because it was never a separate line item in SAP. Search, CMS and preview are the usual ones.

Process mapping and unresolved business rules

Data mapping tells you what moves. Process mapping tells you whether the business still works afterwards. The register below is the single most useful artefact in the programme: every open rule has an owner and a deadline.

Open ruleWhy unresolvedDecidesNeeded by
Promotion stacking and prioritySAP's promotion engine behaviour was never written down; Centra campaigns may not replicate itBrand/Market lead, with FinanceBefore build of campaigns
Staff and VIP discount eligibilityLogic lives in customer groups and custom codeBrand/Market leadBefore pricelist design
Price rounding and tax-inclusive display per marketDiffers by market; inherited, not documentedFinanceBefore UAT
Partial refund and return allocationOrder-level vs line-level handling differsProduct owner, with PaymentsBefore payment config
Guest vs registered checkoutMarkets want different rulesBrand/Market leadBefore frontend build
Mark each process as-is, to-be or retired. Retired processes are a quiet win: some SAP behaviours exist only because of SAP.
Part 2

Validation and readiness

Readiness is evidence, not opinion. Each gate has criteria agreed before anyone is asked to pass it.

GateWhat is checkedEvidence
Mapping sign-offEvery entity flagged; no unowned unresolved rulesSigned mapping document, rule register
Data load rehearsalRecord counts, product-to-size integrity, price totals per pricelist, stock totals per warehouse, customer countsReconciliation report with variances explained
Business UATPer brand and market: browse, basket, promotions, checkout, payment, refundExecuted scripts, defects triaged
Non-functionalLoad, failover, peak behaviourTest results against agreed thresholds
Cutover rehearsalTimed runbook, rollback testedRehearsal log with actual vs planned
Go/no-goAll gates met or consciously acceptedSponsor decision recorded

Stakeholder conflict and challenging assumptions

Conflict in a migration is rarely personal. It is usually two legitimate interests that the old platform let coexist and the new one forces into the open.

Assumption heardWhy it is riskyHow I challenge it
"Centra will behave exactly like SAP"Different promotion, catalog and pricing modelsAsk for the rule as a test case; run it in Centra and show the result, not an opinion
"Search and content come with the platform"Centra is headless; neither is includedShow the scope table; ask who owns and funds each
"We will migrate every order since launch"Cost and reconciliation effort rise with volumeAsk what decision the old orders support; propose an archive plus a defined window
"All brands need their own checkout"Multiplies build, test and supportSeparate position from interest; offer configurable variance inside shared guardrails
Brand vs. central

Differentiation vs. cost

Name the trade-off in money and time. Let the Accountable stakeholder choose with the cost visible.

Finance vs. market

Rounding, tax display, refunds

Put both rules side by side on a real order. Finance decides; the market gets the reasoning.

Product vs. engineering

Attribute model

Show the field in the target. Disagreements about structure dissolve when the data is on screen.

Always

Log the decision

Who, what, why, what was rejected. Most repeat conflicts are decisions nobody wrote down.

When developers say it is technically infeasible

"Cannot" is the start of a conversation, not the end of one. The BA's job is to find out which kind of cannot it is.

VerdictMeaningWhat the BA does next
FeasibleWorks nativelyConfirm acceptance criteria
WorkaroundPossible with configuration or middlewareGet effort and the business trade-off; take options to the owner
Needs vendor or custom buildOutside standard capabilityCost it, ask Centra directly, raise as a scope decision
Not feasibleConstraint is realReframe the requirement: change the process, descope or accept the impact
Ask three questions every time: what exactly is the constraint, what are the options, and what does each cost? Then translate the answer into business impact for the person who owns the decision. Never carry a "no" to the business without options attached.

Governance across brand and market variance

Centra can serve several brands and markets from one instance, which is exactly why variance needs rules. Without them, every market becomes a bespoke project.

LayerExamplesWho decides
Global coreData model, checkout flow, payment standards, securityDesign authority; changes are exceptional
ConfigurableMarkets, pricelists, campaigns, shipping options, languageBrand/Market lead within guardrails
LocalLegal wording, local payment methods, local promotionsMarket lead, with Legal and Compliance
Run a variance register: each request logged, classified into a layer, costed and approved or declined. Review it at a fixed cadence so variance is a decision, not a drift.
Part 3

Escalation and contingency planning

Decide the escalation path and the fallback before they are needed. Nobody designs these well mid-incident.

LevelWhoTime-boxTrigger
L1Workstream leads24hBlocked item, unclear owner
L2Programme manager and product owner48hCross-workstream or cross-brand conflict
L3Steering committeeNext session or ad hocScope, budget, timeline or compliance impact

Escalate with options, not problems: state the issue, the impact, two or three choices with costs, and your recommendation.

RiskTriggerContingency
Data load fails reconciliationVariance above agreed threshold in rehearsalHold go-live; fix and re-rehearse
Critical defect after cutoverOrders or payments failingRollback runbook, tested in rehearsal
Market not readyGate criteria unmetPhased rollout: pilot market first, others follow
Peak trading approachingCutover within the freeze windowMove the date; do not migrate into peak (see the peak vs. non-peak case study)
Legacy platform needed longerParallel running requiredAgreed read-only period and cost approved up front
A rollback plan that was never rehearsed is a hope. Rehearse the rollback as seriously as the cutover.
