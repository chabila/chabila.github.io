A domain-transfer case study

Where payments & eCommerce BA experience meets insurance

Strip away the vocabulary and a policy lifecycle is an order lifecycle; a bordereaux is a reconciliation report; a quote-to-bind funnel is a checkout funnel with an underwriter in the loop instead of a fraud check. This is a detailed look at where my background actually lines up with insurance BA work — and where AI fits once it does.

Chabila Borkar· Senior Business Analyst, Payments & eCommerce
A quick note on how to read this

I haven't shipped a policy administration system. What I have done is spend seven years in the parts of retail technology that turn out to be structurally identical to insurance's core processes: order-to-cash, reconciliation across three systems that don't agree with each other, funnel drop-off analysis, and regulatory compliance work that doesn't forgive a sloppy requirement. This case study is me being honest about exactly where that maps, in the industry's own language, not a generic "transferable skills" gesture.

Part 1

Why this transfers, not just "soft skills"

Insurance and eCommerce payments look unrelated from the outside — one sells cover, one sells products. Underneath, both run on the same three structural problems: a multi-step conversion funnel with drop-off at every stage, a system-of-record that has to reconcile against money actually moving, and a regulatory layer that treats a wrong requirement as a compliance incident, not just a bug.

The domain mapping

Same underlying BA problem, different industry vocabulary. This is the translation table I'd hand a hiring manager who's wondering whether payments experience actually says anything about insurance readiness.

The vocabulary is genuinely different — and worth learning properly, not glossing over. But the analytical instinct is the same one I've used for seven years: find where two systems disagree about a status or an amount, and fix the requirement that let them disagree.
Part 2 — Across the policy lifecycle

New business: quote to bind

The insurance equivalent of a checkout funnel, with an underwriter standing where a fraud check would sit.

WHAT'S HAPPENING
What a BA is doing

Specifying rating inputs and underwriting referral rules

Defining what data the rating engine needs, which answers trigger an automatic decline or refer-to-underwriter, and what a compliant IPID (Insurance Product Information Document) has to contain before a quote can even be shown.

Where AI earns its keep

Drafting a first-pass referral rule table from underwriting guidelines, and summarising quote-abandonment analytics into candidate friction points — the same shape of work as checkout drop-off analysis, aimed at a different funnel.

Policy administration & billing

Where the policy record has to stay perfectly in sync with what the customer actually agreed to — and what they're actually being charged.

WHAT'S HAPPENING
What a BA is doing

Defining endorsement and instalment billing rules

Specifying pro-rata premium recalculation on a mid-term adjustment, direct debit failure and grace-period handling, and how a missed payment eventually triggers cancellation without breaching Consumer Duty fair-treatment expectations.

Where AI earns its keep

Turning a PAS vendor's endorsement API docs into a first-pass field mapping — structurally identical to PSP integration mapping — and flagging bordereaux reconciliation mismatches across thousands of broker-reported lines for investigation.

Claims

The moment the product is actually delivered — and the stage where a slow or wrong decision does the most reputational damage.

WHAT'S HAPPENING
What a BA is doing

Specifying FNOL intake, triage, and reserve-setting rules

Defining what information FNOL capture needs, the severity/complexity rules that route a claim to straight-through processing versus a loss adjuster, and the reserve-setting logic that estimates likely payout early.

Where AI earns its keep

Summarising a loss adjuster's free-text notes into a structured case file, and clustering historic claims by likely fraud indicators to support (never replace) an SIU referral decision — genuinely high-leverage, and exactly where the human decision matters most.

Renewals & retention

Where UK insurance regulation changed the rules more than almost any other stage in the last few years.

WHAT'S HAPPENING
What a BA is doing

Specifying re-rating and fair-value renewal pricing checks

Since the FCA's price-walking ban, a renewal price can no longer exceed what an equivalent new customer would pay — which means the renewal engine needs an explicit fair-value check built in, not bolted on afterwards.

Where AI earns its keep

Drafting the fair-value comparison logic from the regulatory requirement, and summarising retention/lapse patterns into candidate causes — the renewal-stage equivalent of customer-journey retention analysis in eCommerce.

Before you touch a model

What holds true across the insurance value chain

01

Policy wording is the contract, not a guideline

Any AI-drafted answer touching coverage or claims has to be checked against the actual contractual wording. This is a stricter version of "AI drafts, human verifies" than most of payments — the wrong answer here can be a breach, not just an error.

02

The bordereaux is where the money actually reconciles

MGA, broker, and insurer relationships live or die on an accurate bordereaux. It's the direct insurance analogue of settlement reconciliation in payments — same discipline, different document.

03

Consumer Duty changed the renewal playbook

Insurers can no longer charge loyal customers more than new ones at renewal. Any renewal pricing requirement written today has to build the fair-value check in from the start, not retrofit it.

04

Claims data often carries sensitive personal data

Health and injury details in claims files mean data-handling obligations here are frequently heavier than general eCommerce PII, closer in weight to healthcare data governance.

Part 3 — Where AI embeds

The same three techniques, applied to a stricter contract

RAG, human-in-the-loop, and MCP servers show up across the whole AI-in-BA series — insurance just raises the stakes on all three, because the artefact a model might get wrong is a binding contract, not a product description.

Grounding

Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Ground the model in actual policy wordings, the underwriting manual, and the FCA Handbook sections that apply — not general insurance knowledge. The difference between a plausible-sounding coverage answer and one that cites the specific exclusion clause in the actual policy document.

Maps to: claims & underwriting — where an ungrounded answer risks misstating a contractual term.
Control

Human-in-the-loop

Underwriting decisions above a sum-insured threshold, claims settlement above a delegated authority limit, and any customer complaint — these are the checkpoints where a named human has to sign off, full stop, no matter how confident the model is.

Maps to: underwriting authority limits & complaints — where FCA accountability rules mean a person, not a model, owns the decision.
Connection

MCP servers

An agent connected live to the PAS (Guidewire, Duck Creek, Sapiens), the claims management system, and the broker portal can pull a policy's actual current status and claims history — instead of a BA stitching together three exports to answer one question.

Maps to: policy servicing & claims handling — live status across systems that rarely talk to each other natively.
Part 4 — The regulatory layer

Why insurance BA work carries a heavier compliance load

Payments has PSD2 and PCI-DSS. Insurance stacks a prudential and conduct regime on top of its own version of both — this is the layer that makes "AI drafts, human verifies" non-negotiable rather than best practice.

SOLVENCY II

Capital adequacy & technical provisions

Sets how much capital an insurer must hold against its risks. BA work touching reserving or pricing has to respect the technical provisions framework underneath it.

IFRS 17

Insurance contract accounting

A major recent overhaul of how insurance contracts are recognised and measured financially — any requirement touching premium recognition timing now has to account for it.

FCA CONSUMER DUTY

Fair value & the price-walking ban

Requires products to deliver fair value and bans charging renewing customers more than equivalent new customers — directly shapes renewal pricing requirements.

IDD

Insurance Distribution Directive

Governs how products are sold and disclosed, including the IPID requirement at point of quote — a direct input into new-business requirements.

GDPR & SENSITIVE DATA

Health and injury data in claims

Claims files routinely contain special-category data under GDPR, raising the bar on access controls and retention rules beyond general eCommerce PII handling.

FCA / PRA

Conduct & prudential regulation

The FCA governs conduct (how customers are treated) while the PRA governs prudential soundness (can the insurer pay claims) — two different regulators, two different sets of requirements to satisfy simultaneously.

Part 5 — Hands-on

Building this in Microsoft Foundry

The sequence I'd follow to build an assistant that supports insurance BA work end to end, respecting the stricter contractual and regulatory stakes. Microsoft Foundry (formerly Azure AI Foundry), current as of 2026.

Create a Foundry project and connect policy and regulatory documents

Stand up a project, then connect policy wordings, the underwriting manual, and relevant FCA Handbook sections — via Azure Blob Storage or a data connection — ready to be indexed.

Deploy a model from the catalog

Pick a model (e.g. gpt-4o) from the Foundry model catalog as your reasoning engine — swappable later without rebuilding the rest.

Ground it against your own policy wordings (RAG)

Index the connected documents with Azure AI Search, add it to your agent as a knowledge source, so any coverage or claims answer cites the actual clause it's drawing from.

Attach live systems via MCP

From the Foundry portal's Tools menu, connect the PAS and claims management system as tool connections, so the agent can pull a specific policy's live status and claims history rather than working from a stale export.

Add human-approval checkpoints at underwriting and claims authority limits

Flag any decision above a defined sum-insured or settlement authority threshold as requiring approval. The workflow pauses on a function_approval_request and only proceeds once a named underwriter or claims handler signs off.

Orchestrate it as a lifecycle-aware workflow

Use Foundry's workflow builder to chain retrieval → drafting → human review across new business, servicing, and claims as distinct stages within one workflow, not three disconnected tools.

Evaluate before you trust it, then monitor

Run Foundry's built-in groundedness and relevance evaluations before rollout — especially critical here, given the contractual stakes — then use tracing and Application Insights to monitor what it's actually doing once live.
